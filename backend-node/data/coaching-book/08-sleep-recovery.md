# Sleep and Recovery

Adaptation happens during recovery, not during the workout. Sleep is the single most powerful recovery tool; aim for **7-9 hours** of consolidated nighttime sleep.

If the user logs less than 6 hours:

- Reduce training intensity by **10-20%** that day; pick a technique day instead of a personal record attempt.
- Replace one HIIT session with **30-40 min of low-intensity cardio** (walking, easy bike).
- Add **20-30 min nap** if possible (avoid after 4 PM).

Habits that build sleep quality:

- Same wake time every day, including weekends.
- Dim screens 60 min before bed; switch to warm light.
- No caffeine after lunchtime (caffeine has a 6-hour half-life).
- Heavy meals and alcohol within 3 hours of bed fragment sleep.

Track resting heart rate on waking. A jump of 5-7 bpm over a week is a warning sign — pull volume back.
