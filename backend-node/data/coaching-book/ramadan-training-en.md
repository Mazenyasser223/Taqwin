# Training during Ramadan

Ramadan is not a reason to stop training — it is a reason to **adjust** timing, intensity, and hydration so you keep muscle and energy without overstressing your body.

## Best workout timing

- **1–2 hours after iftar:** Best for most people — you have eaten and hydrated; you can lift meaningful loads.
- **Before suhoor:** Works if evenings are impossible; keep sessions **lighter** (45–60 minutes, moderate intensity).
- **Avoid:** Hard training while fasted — higher risk of dizziness, dehydration, and poor performance.

## Volume and intensity

- Reduce **weekly volume** by 20–40% in the first two weeks if you feel flat.
- Keep **main lifts** but with fewer sets: 3×6–8 instead of 4×8–10.
- **Cardio:** Minimal — light walk after taraweeh or 15 minutes cycling post-iftar is enough for most goals.

## Nutrition and hydration

- **Iftar:** Protein + moderate carbs + vegetables — do not binge huge portions immediately.
- **Suhoor:** Slow-digesting protein (eggs, yogurt, cheese) and plenty of water.
- **Supplements:** Take creatine or whey after iftar if part of your plan — not mid-fast.

## Taqwin life_mode

- Enable **life mode: fasting** in coach or settings — plans adapt (fewer meals, shorter sessions).
- If traveling during Ramadan, combine with **travel** mode — hotel gym or bodyweight; consistency beats PRs.

## When to rest

- Dizziness, headaches, or sharp performance drops → **rest day** or deload week.
- Coach is not a doctor — acute pain or chronic conditions: get medical clearance before continuing.
